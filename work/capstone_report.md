Abstract:
This case study addresses a core FlyRank content problem: "Seen But Ignored" pages that rank highly but suffer from abnormally low click-through rates (CTR). By framing this as a classification task on trailing 90-day search data, I replaced rigid static rules with a Random Forest model. The resulting system improved recommendation precision by over 3x, providing content teams with a targeted, data-backed queue for meta-tag rewrites.

Introduction:
Content and SEO teams often have limited bandwidth and waste hours guessing which underperforming pages to update first. Fixing metadata on a page that is already performing optimally for its intent can actually damage rankings. This project provides decision support by using historical search and engagement signals to mathematically identify which top-ranking pages are most likely suffering from poor titles or descriptions. By surfacing these anomalies, editors only spend time where the opportunity for traffic recovery is highest.
